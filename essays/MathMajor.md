While I will be graduating in the Summer of 2026, I’ve finished all my math class requirements including math research in the Spring semester. Although I didn’t necessarily intend to be a math major at first, I found that I was always interested in the new things that I was learning within Math, and that I had made some unexpected friends within the community. I chose Math as a major because I thought it would give me the broadest base, and open the door to many different tracks for a future graduate degree and career. To be honest, before starting a math research project of my own, I wasn’t even sure what the possibilities were, and I wasn’t very interested in formal proof writing. However, I was considering going into either Math Education, Computer Engineering, Data Management, or Statistical Work.

## The Fun

During my time at the University of Hawaii at Manoa, I got a position as a Learning Assistant and helped many people in the lower-level Math classes, such as MATH 134 (Precalculus: Elementary Functions) and MATH 140X (Precalculus). From previous tutoring experiences in middle school and high school, I knew that I would really like the Learning Assistant experience as well. Part of me also thinks that I did get very lucky with both my student population, my boss, as well as the Teaching Assistants and Instructors that I worked with.

<img class="img-fluid" src="../img/mathmajor.jpg">

## The Difficult

However, I definitely had a much more difficult time getting adjusted to formal proof writing, which was the majority of my work within the higher-level math classes. It was difficult to adapt to the styles of each new professor and sometimes it felt like the math classes were more like English classes. However, the community of undergraduate students within math, whether they’re adjacent, minoring, majoring, or double majoring in math, is a very tight-knit, sweet community. There were so many times where we would all be struggling with something, and this created bonds of friendship as we could relate to each other. The graduate student community is very kind as well. Although I would try not to bother them with questions, any time I did ask a question, even if it was a very simple one, they would happily answer it for me.

## The Takeaway

Overall, I’ve really enjoyed my experience at University of Hawaii at Manoa within the Math Department. I feel like I’ve made many important professional and personal connections with kind faculty members and students.
