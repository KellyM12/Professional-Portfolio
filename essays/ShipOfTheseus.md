## The Ship of Theseus

Whenever I think of working on a significant project, especially with other people, I always think of the Ship of Theseus. In the original experiment of the Ship of Theseus, the question is whether an object remains fundamentally the same object if it has had all of its components replaced, little by little over time. My using the analogy of the Ship of Theseus to a group project is related to how the ship gets built over time, with constant replacements and improvements. At first, the hull skeleton of the ship needs to be built to establish a sort of structure and theme. Then, using that support, more of the ship can be constructed as a draft to get an outline for the very necessary ideas that eventually become the important components. These include things like beds in which people sleep, food storage, and how the ship will navigate in the sea speedily. Once this draft of the ship is built, there are considerations for optimizing these processes further. What kind of bedding, food, and tools will be needed and considered for the journey? After the questions of improving function have been answered, you need to select paints and embellishments to decorate the ship, for the aesthetics and theming. However, even if the ship is ready for the journey, it may still need replacements and improvements. Components that were used in previous places have been found to be better suited for a different use. New components that were floating in the sea or found during a stop help get the ship to function perfectly. The ship becomes more and more optimized, with everyone trying to collaborate together on how it can best suit their needs. This is very similar to a well-organized group project.

## The Project

Whenever working on a significant project in any field, it’s important to consider how each of the individual components will work with each other, and help to optimize each other. Design patterns operate in this sort of manner, as there are many different thoughts for how software engineers can best optimize the building process of any given project. Coding can often be tedious, so if it can be done efficiently and flexible enough to be reused, this not only saves a lot of time but also helps keep the project more consistent as a whole.

Our final project is “Outside Buddies”, which has the intention of helping people who are excited to try hiking in Hawaii connect with others, so that they’re not alone. This would help hikers and other outdoorsy people create a community. This involves many people interacting with each other, and most people will probably want to get information in a similar manner. For considering different groups to join, users will want to know what the goal of the group is, the number of members, and the types of events that they host. For looking at individual profiles, users will care more about their goals and descriptions (things like aptitude, experience, skill level, etc.). While the individual information itself is a bit different, the way in which it will be displayed is relatively similar. Outside Buddies also allows for groups (created by users) and administrators to post their own hiking events. The forms that users and administrators will need to use to enter information will be very similar to each other. For me, I believe this best matches the Observer Pattern idea of Design Patterns, as groups will be able to update their own information and events, users can update their profiles to best describe their interests, and administrators will be providing community events for people who want to try the experience.

## The Everchanging Ship (or Project)

What’s great about the Ship of Theseus is that at first, the process can feel very confusing. As many ideas are proposed and decisions are made, sometimes it feels like taking 1 step forwards and 2 steps backwards. However, that is what makes the process so fun and rewarding; doing it together as a team. Depending on the team’s passion and the type of project, it can also continue on forever; voyaging the seven seas whilst collecting new things and trying new experiences with everyone who interacts with your ship. These small beginnings could lead to new friendships and community.
