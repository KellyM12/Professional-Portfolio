

Whenever I think of working on a significant project, especially with other people, I always think of the Ship of Theseus. Not necessarily in the paradoxical questioning if it’s the same ship sort of way, but more like how the ship gets built over time. At first, the hull skeleton of the ship needs to be built to establish a sort of structure and theme. Then, using that support more of the ship can be constructed as a draft to get an outline for the very necessary ideas that eventually become the important components, things like beds for people to sleep, food storage for the people on the ship, and how the ship will navigate in the sea speedily. Once this draft of the ship is built, there are considerations for optimizing these processes further. What kind of bedding, food, and tools will be needed and considered for the journey? After the questions of improving function, paints and embellishments to decorate up the ship, for the aesthetic and theming. However, even if the ship is ready for the journey, it may still need improving even after. Components that were used in previous places have been found for a better use case. New components that were floating in the sea or found during a stop are for the perfect function. The ship becomes more and more optimized, with everyone trying to collaborate together on how it can best suit their needs. 

Whenever working on a significant project in any field, it’s important to consider how each of the individual components will work with each other and how to optimize making any potential components if they could be similar to each other. Design patterns operate in this sort of manner, as there are many different thoughts for how software engineers can best optimize the building process of any given project. Coding can often be tedious, so if it can be efficiently made and flexible enough to be reused, this saves not only a lot of time but also helps keep the project more consistent as a whole.

Our final project is Outside Buddies, which has the intention of helping people excited to try hiking in Hawaii connect with others so that they’re not alone and provide a community. This involves many people interacting with each other, and most people will probably want to know information in a similar manner. For considering different groups to join, users will want to know what the goal of the group is, the number of members, and the types of events that they host. For looking at individual profiles, users will care more about their goals and descriptions (things like aptitude, experience, etc.). While the individual information itself is a bit different, the way in which it will be displayed is relatively similar. Outside Buddies also allows for groups (created by users) and admins to post their own hiking events. The forms that users and admin will need to use to enter information will be very similar to each other, as most users will want similar information in both instances. To me, I believe this best matches the Observer Pattern idea of Design Patterns, as groups will be able to update with their own information and events, users can update their profiles to best describe their interests, and admins will be providing community events for people who want to know what it can be like.

What’s great about the Ship of Theseus, is that at first the process can feel very confusing. As many ideas are proposed and decisions are made, sometimes it feels like taking 1 step forwards 2 steps backwards. However, that is what makes the process so fun and rewarding; doing it together as a team. Depending on the team’s passion and the project, it can also continue on forever; voyaging the seven seas whilst collecting new things and trying new experiences with everyone who interacts with your ship that started small.
